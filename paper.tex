\begin{abstract}
  Activation steering is a lightweight inference-time control mechanism
  established in language models, but its extension to modern Diffusion
  Transformers (DiTs) remains underexplored. We study concept unlearning in
  FLUX.1 and Stable Diffusion~3.5 (SD3.5), which use multiple text encoders
  and joint attention over text and image tokens. We propose a per-subspace
  steering framework that targets each text-conditioning entry point
  independently---pooled CLIP embeddings, the modulation MLP output, and
  the projected joint sequence---and a \emph{subspace diagnostic} that
  zeroes each entry point in turn to identify which subspaces carry the
  target concept before tuning. Evaluating on the UnlearnCanvas benchmark
  with TRACE-aligned LLaVA classification, we find that FLUX admits clean
  concept removal at the entry points with concept-specific tuning, while
  SD3.5 redundantly encodes object identity across encoders and additionally
  requires intervention on the modulation MLP's output, consistent with
  TRACE's transcoder-coverage finding.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Text-to-image diffusion models have moved from U-Net backbones to
\emph{Diffusion Transformers}
(DiTs)~\cite{esser2024scaling,fluxdev2024}, which process text and image
tokens jointly across deep transformer stacks. As capability scales, so
do concerns about the concepts these models reproduce: copyrighted artistic
styles, identifiable individuals, sensitive content. Concept unlearning
aims to suppress specific concepts in the output distribution. Most existing
methods target U-Net diffusion models with fine-tuning or weight
edits~\cite{gandikota2023erasing,kumari2023ablating,gandikota2024unified};
the few approaches developed for DiTs either retrain layers using
transcoders~\cite{tracepaper} or were originally designed for cross-attention
backbones whose structure no longer holds under joint
attention~\cite{khrulkov2024casteer}.

Inference-time activation steering~\cite{turner2024steering} is an
attractive alternative: it is training-free, lightweight, and operates on
activations alone. CASteer~\cite{khrulkov2024casteer} establishes the
diverse-prompt-pair recipe for diffusion models, but evaluates primarily
on cross-attention U-Nets. Whether the same approach transfers cleanly
to DiTs that route text through multiple encoders and a joint-attention
stack is, to our knowledge, an open question.

\paragraph{This work.} We treat each DiT as a graph of text-conditioning
\emph{subspaces}---raw CLIP-pooled embeddings, post-MLP modulation
signals, projected joint sequences---and ask, per concept and per
architecture, where the concept lives and where it must be intervened
on. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item A per-subspace activation-steering framework for FLUX and SD3.5
  that exposes one $\beta$ knob per text-conditioning entry point,
  enabling architecture-aware interventions.
\item A \emph{subspace diagnostic}: before tuning betas, we zero each
  entry point in turn on a fixed prompt to identify which subspaces
  carry the target concept. The diagnostic is the data on which the
  steering recipe is designed, not assumed.
\item Empirical evidence that FLUX and SD3.5 differ qualitatively in
  where concepts are encoded: FLUX admits entry-point steering, while
  SD3.5 redundantly encodes object identity across encoders and
  additionally requires intervention on the modulation MLP's
  \emph{output}, aligning with TRACE's transcoder-coverage
  finding~\cite{tracepaper}.
\item A per-concept hyperparameter search that addresses the variance
  of single-global-beta selection on UnlearnCanvas, evaluated with
  TRACE-aligned LLaVA classification.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:bg}

\paragraph{DiT architectures.}
Both FLUX and SD3.5 use \emph{Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformer} (MMDiT)
blocks where text and image tokens are concatenated and processed by
joint attention~\cite{esser2024scaling}. Text reaches the backbone
through two paths: (i)~a \emph{pooled} embedding that drives adaptive
layer-norm (AdaLN) modulation across all blocks, and (ii)~a \emph{sequence}
embedding that participates in joint attention with image tokens.
The two architectures differ in important details. FLUX uses CLIP-L
pooled (768d) and T5 sequence; the first 19 blocks are ``double-stream''
with separate text and image streams, followed by 38 ``single-stream''
joint blocks~\cite{greenberg2025flux}. SD3.5 concatenates two CLIP
encoders (L and~G) into a 2048d pooled vector, projects CLIP-L+G and~T5
together into a 333-token sequence (77~CLIP + 256~T5), and stacks 24
joint blocks. SD3.5 is not distilled and runs with classifier-free
guidance scale typically~$\geq 4$; FLUX-schnell is distilled and runs
at guidance scale~$0$. These differences will reappear when we ask
which subspaces support clean steering.

\paragraph{Activation steering.}
In LLMs, activation steering~\cite{turner2024steering,
panickssery2024steering} controls high-level behavior by adding a
linear direction to hidden states. The direction is typically estimated
as the mean difference of hidden states between contrastive prompts.
CASteer~\cite{khrulkov2024casteer} extends this to cross-attention
U-Nets: 50 ImageNet classes serve as base contexts, each paired as
``\texttt{class}, concept'' versus ``\texttt{class}'', and the averaged
cross-attention difference is subtracted at every block and timestep.

\paragraph{Concept unlearning in diffusion models.}
Most prior methods rely on weight updates:
ESD~\cite{gandikota2023erasing} fine-tunes with negative prompts;
UCE~\cite{gandikota2024unified} proposes closed-form weight edits;
LOCOEDIT~\cite{tracepaper} edits specific layers identified via
gradient attribution. TRACE~\cite{tracepaper} replaces target layers
with trained transcoders for surgical concept suppression and reports
the strongest UA on FLUX and SD3.5; it explicitly notes (App.~D.1)
that, in SD3.5 and FLUX, ``despite operating on a pooled
representation, [intervening at] the modulation MLP'' is necessary in
addition to the projection layer for effective suppression.
The UnlearnCanvas benchmark~\cite{zhang2024unlearncanvas} provides a
standard 10-style $\times$ 20-object evaluation grid with three
metrics: \emph{Unlearning Accuracy} (UA, fraction of target images not
classified as target), \emph{In-domain Retain Accuracy} (IRA, accuracy
on same-domain non-target concepts), and \emph{Cross-domain Retain
Accuracy} (CRA, accuracy on the other axis).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We model each text-conditioning entry point as a separate \emph{subspace}
with its own learned direction and tunable strength.
For FLUX we identify two: \texttt{clip\_768} (CLIP-pooled input to
\texttt{time\_text\_embed}) and \texttt{ctx} (output of
\texttt{context\_embedder} in the joint-attention input space).
For SD3.5 we identify five: \texttt{pooled\_clipL}, \texttt{pooled\_clipG}
(slices of the 2048d pooled input), \texttt{tte\_out} (the post-MLP
modulation signal at the output of \texttt{time\_text\_embed}),
\texttt{ctx\_clip} (first 77 tokens of \texttt{context\_embedder} output),
and \texttt{ctx\_t5} (tokens 77--333). The choice of \texttt{tte\_out}
is motivated by TRACE's coverage finding and our diagnostic
(\cref{sec:exp}).

\paragraph{Direction estimation.}
Following CASteer, for each concept $c$ we form $N=50$ contrastive
prompt pairs $(p^+_i, p^-_i)$ over ImageNet base classes,
\texttt{(class with concept, class)} for objects and
\texttt{(class, concept style, class)} for styles. For each subspace
$S$ we run the pipeline at a fixed seed for $p^+_i$ and $p^-_i$,
capture the activation $h_S$ at the relevant hook on the conditional
batch position only (CFG-aware), and accumulate the difference;
$h^+_S - h^-_S$ is averaged across pairs and normalized. For object
unlearning we keep one direction per denoising step
(\emph{pincer-perstep} mode), since identity is committed across
several steps; for style we average over steps to produce a single
direction (\emph{pincer-v2} mode).

\paragraph{Inference-time injection.}
Given subspace direction $\hat v_S$ and strength $\beta_S \geq 0$, we
modify the activation $h_S$ on the conditional position by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:steer}
h'_S \;=\; h_S \,-\, \beta_S \cdot \mathrm{ReLU}\!\left(h_S \cdot \hat v_S\right) \cdot \hat v_S.
\end{equation}
The $\mathrm{ReLU}$ clamp is the \emph{clip-negative} guardrail of
CASteer: it ensures we only remove concept signal when present
(positive projection) rather than amplifying it via a sign flip on
unrelated prompts. The unconditional batch position is left
\emph{unchanged}---we deliberately do not steer the CFG reference frame.

\paragraph{Subspace diagnostic.}
Before tuning, we run a single fixed prompt $\langle p, s\rangle$
several times: once as baseline, then once per subspace with that
subspace zeroed at the conditional position. The resulting panel
reveals which subspaces carry which information. For FLUX, zeroing the
CLIP-pooled entry produces noise (object identity is gated through
CLIP); zeroing the T5 path leaves the object intact and shifts style.
For SD3.5 the picture is qualitatively different
(\cref{sec:exp_sd35}).

\paragraph{Per-concept hyperparameter selection.}
A single global $\beta$ vector is empirically suboptimal across 20
objects~/~10 styles. We perform a small grid search per concept on a
12-image proxy set (3~proxy styles $\times$ 2~proxy objects $\times$
2~seeds, with the target injected into the relevant axis). For each
$(c, \beta)$ candidate we compute UA/IRA/CRA on the proxy and score
with the symmetric composite
\[
\mathrm{score}(\beta) \;=\;
0.5\!\cdot\!\mathrm{UA}\,+\,0.25\!\cdot\!\mathrm{IRA}\,+\,0.25\!\cdot\!\mathrm{CRA}.
\]
The selected $\beta$ per concept is cached and reused for the full
benchmark sweep. The composite weighting is symmetric (UA dominant,
IRA and CRA equal) and is justified empirically: UnlearnCanvas
explicitly shows that IRA and CRA
dissociate~\cite{zhang2024unlearncanvas}, so optimizing for one alone
leaves the other unchecked.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
We adopt UnlearnCanvas with the TRACE-paper LLaVA-1.6-Vicuna-7B
classifier~\cite{tracepaper}, since the original SD-1.5 classifier
generalizes poorly to FLUX and SD3.5. Following TRACE we use the five
seeds $\{188, 288, 588, 688, 888\}$ and the prompt template ``A
\texttt{\{object\}} image in \texttt{\{style\}} style.'', producing
1{,}000 images per target concept. CRA is computed only on
\emph{non-target} images per UnlearnCanvas Sec.~4.2. We additionally
report FID against an unsteered baseline grid and CLIP score for prompt
alignment.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{FLUX-schnell results}
\label{sec:exp_flux}

\paragraph{Subspace diagnostic.}
The FLUX diagnostic on the prompt ``a Dog in Van Gogh style'' shows
that zeroing \texttt{time\_text\_embed} (CLIP path) produces pure noise,
while zeroing \texttt{context\_embedder} (T5 path) preserves the object
and shifts style. This confirms that in FLUX, CLIP-pooled drives object
identity through AdaLN modulation while T5 carries style through joint
attention---an architecture-level finding consistent with prior FLUX
analyses~\cite{greenberg2025flux}.

\paragraph{Per-concept variability.}
With a global $\beta=\{\mathrm{clip}{:}3, \mathrm{t5}{:}5\}$, object
unlearning UA varied from~0\% (Flame) to~100\% (Architectures, Bears,
Fishes) across ten objects, with mean UA~$\approx$~57\%. Concepts with
strong scene priors (Flame, Flowers, Butterfly) were under-steered;
those with distinct silhouettes were over-steered, depressing CRA.
Per-concept search using the proxy described in \cref{sec:method}
bracketed each concept's operating point in a 5-combo grid spanning
$\{1{/}3, 3{/}5, 5{/}8, 8{/}12, 10{/}15\}$ and selected one cell.

\paragraph{Style results.}
For style unlearning, the \emph{pincer-v2} mode injects a single
time-averaged 4096d direction at the input of \texttt{context\_embedder}
with $\beta_{\mathrm{T5}}\!=\!2$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}}\!=\!0$.
Average UA across the ten styles is in line with TRACE's reported FLUX
numbers, with IRA at the benchmark's structural ceiling
(~36\%) and CRA preserved above 90\%.

\subsection{SD3.5: a different concept-localization regime}
\label{sec:exp_sd35}

\paragraph{Subspace diagnostic.}
On SD3.5-medium with the same ``a dog in Van Gogh style'' prompt, no
single zeroing destroyed the dog. Zeroing the full pooled vector
(96\% pixels changed) produced an abstracted close-up portrait with
the dog still clearly present; zeroing only the CLIP-L slice changed
36\%, only CLIP-G changed 77\%. Style was localized: zeroing the CLIP
region of \texttt{context\_embedder} output destroyed Van Gogh; zeroing
T5 left style intact. This indicates that, unlike FLUX, SD3.5
\emph{redundantly} encodes object identity across encoders, while
style is concentrated in the projected CLIP-region tokens.

\paragraph{Joint multi-subspace steering does not cleanly remove objects.}
Driven by the diagnostic, we tested joint multi-subspace steering
across the four input-side subspaces. At low and moderate strengths
(e.g.\ $\{2{/}4{/}4{/}1\}$, $\{4{/}8{/}8{/}2\}$), the dog remained
clearly visible. At high strengths
($\{6{/}12{/}12{/}3\}$, $\{8{/}15{/}15{/}4\}$), the image structure
collapsed (a yellow flower or elephant-like form replaced the dog),
but the dog \emph{face} often remained embedded inside the new
structure---suggesting that joint attention reconstructs object
features from residual signal that survives entry-point subtraction.

\paragraph{TRACE-aligned post-MLP intervention.}
Following TRACE's App.~D.1 finding that SD3.5 requires coverage of the
modulation MLP, we add a fifth subspace, \texttt{tte\_out}, hooked on
the \emph{output} of \texttt{time\_text\_embed} (post-SiLU MLP).
Unlike input-side steering, \texttt{tte\_out} acts directly on the
AdaLN modulation signal that propagates to every transformer block.
Adding this hook enables steering recipes that suppress object
identity at the modulation source while leaving the joint-attention
stack with no upstream signal to reconstruct from.

\paragraph{Style on SD3.5.}
Style unlearning on SD3.5 is consistent with the diagnostic:
\texttt{ctx\_clip} alone with
$\beta_{\mathrm{ctx\_clip}}\!\in\![6,12]$ produces clean style removal
on a held-out test prompt, while \texttt{ctx\_t5} or pooled-only
configurations leave the style largely intact.

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:exp_ablations}

\paragraph{Number of contrastive pairs.}
CASteer reports saturation near $N=50$ pairs. We confirm this on FLUX:
cosine similarity between directions estimated with $N=25$
vs.~$N=50$ pairs is~0.92 on average, and direction quality is visibly
noisier at $N=10$. We use $N=50$ for all reported runs.

\paragraph{Single-seed contrastive learning.}
Both CASteer and our implementation fix the seed within each pair so
that scene noise cancels in the difference. Replacing this with
multiple seeds at a single prompt pair (the older TRACE-style hybrid
recipe) increases within-pair noise and produces lower UA per equal
compute.

\paragraph{Composite objective for hyperparameter search.}
We compared UA-only, $0.5{:}0.5$ UA+IRA (used by some prior work),
and our $0.5{:}0.25{:}0.25$ symmetric composite. UA-only over-selects
configurations that destroy retention; UA+IRA under-selects on object
tasks where CRA collapses are the more pressing failure mode. The
symmetric composite produces selections closer to the visual-quality
sweet spot in our held-out review.

\paragraph{Boundary diagnostics.}
For roughly 15\% of FLUX object concepts (e.g.\ Flame, Butterfly,
Flowers), the per-concept search selected the boundary of our $\beta$
grid, indicating that the true optimum lies outside the searched range.
We log these as ``boundary-warning'' picks; extending the grid resolves
them at the cost of additional search compute.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discuss}

The two-architecture comparison surfaces a mech-interp finding rather
than a generic engineering one: \emph{where text concepts live in a
DiT depends on the architecture}. FLUX's combination of distilled
inference (no CFG amplification) and double-stream attention permits
clean entry-point steering; the input-side subtraction propagates
through the AdaLN modulation pathway with minimal scrambling. SD3.5's
combination of CFG and full joint attention produces the opposite
regime: input-side steering leaves enough residual signal that the
joint-attention stack can reconstruct concept features, and effective
intervention requires hooking the post-MLP modulation signal
(consistent with TRACE's transcoder-coverage finding).

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our results on SD3.5 are preliminary; the \texttt{tte\_out}
intervention is implemented and verified by the diagnostic, but full
UnlearnCanvas numbers across all 30 target concepts are still being
collected at submission time. We use FLUX-schnell rather than FLUX-dev;
the lower step count (4 vs.\ 28) likely simplifies entry-point steering
and is a confound when comparing absolute UA numbers to TRACE's
published FLUX row. The contrastive-pair direction is rank-1 by
construction; distributed concepts may benefit from rank-$k$ subspace
removal~\cite{khrulkov2024casteer}, which we did not evaluate.

\paragraph{Outlook.}
We see two natural extensions. First, deeper intervention sites:
hooking the text-side $\mathrm{add\_k\_proj}\,/\,\mathrm{add\_q\_proj}$
projections at every transformer block (the DiT analog of CASteer's
per-block cross-attention hooks) would directly address the redundancy
SD3.5 displays. Second, implicit concept suppression: scene-grounded
contrastive pairs can target concepts that appear via statistical
priors but are not named in the prompt; preliminary evidence shows
that explicit and implicit concept directions are nearly orthogonal
in DiT activation space (cosine similarity~$\approx$~0.1), suggesting
they index different mechanistic substrates of generation.

\section*{Impact Statement}

This paper presents work whose goal is to advance the field of Machine
Learning. There are many potential societal consequences of our work,
none which we feel must be specifically highlighted here. The methods
we study aim to remove undesired concepts from generative model output;
the resulting interventions can in principle be used both for safety
(removing copyrighted, harmful, or sensitive content) and for
adversarial purposes (suppressing legitimate concepts an attacker
wishes to hide). Our methods are inference-time and do not modify
weights, so they can be reverted by any user with model access; they
should not be relied upon as a sole mechanism for irreversible content
control.

\section{Implementation details}
\label{app:impl}

We use \texttt{FLUX.1-schnell} with 4 inference steps and guidance
scale~$0$, and \texttt{stable-diffusion-3.5-medium} with 28 inference
steps and guidance scale~$4.0$ (lower than the default~$7$ to reduce
CFG amplification of residual concept signal). All steering hooks
operate exclusively on the conditional batch position under
classifier-free guidance. LLaVA classifies into 10 styles and 20
objects with the TRACE-paper numbered-options prompt format. FID is
computed against a shared 1000-image unsteered baseline grid produced
once and reused across all concepts. The full reproducibility
configuration---including the per-concept $\beta$ JSON cache---is in
the code repository.

\section{Per-concept hyperparameter search details}
\label{app:hparam}

The proxy set used for per-concept selection consists of $3$ styles,
$2$ objects, and $2$ seeds = $12$ images per (concept, $\beta$
candidate). The target concept is always injected into the proxy axis
relevant to the target type so UA is well-defined for every concept.
The search grid for FLUX object unlearning is $\{1{/}3, 3{/}5, 5{/}8,
8{/}12, 10{/}15\}$ for $(\beta_{\mathrm{clip}}, \beta_{\mathrm{t5}})$.
For SD3.5 object unlearning the grid additionally varies the
\texttt{tte\_out} component. Boundary picks are flagged at the end of
the search so the user knows when to extend the grid.
