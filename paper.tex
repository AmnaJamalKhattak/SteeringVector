\begin{abstract}
Activation steering, established as a lightweight post-hoc control
mechanism in large language models, has only recently been extended to
diffusion models, and almost exclusively to U-Net architectures. We
study the problem of inference-time concept unlearning in modern
\emph{Diffusion Transformers} (DiTs), focusing on FLUX.1-schnell and
extending preliminary diagnostics to Stable Diffusion~3.5 (SD3.5).
We propose a per-subspace activation-steering framework that targets
each text-conditioning entry point independently---the CLIP-pooled
embedding feeding AdaLN modulation, and the projected T5 sequence
entering joint attention. A \emph{subspace diagnostic} that zeroes
each entry point in turn isolates where object identity and style
live, and the steering recipe is read off this diagnostic rather than
assumed. With per-concept hyperparameter selection on the UnlearnCanvas
benchmark and TRACE-aligned LLaVA classification, our method
(\emph{pincer-v2} for style, \emph{pincer-perstep} for objects)
matches the published FLUX style-removal UA of TRACE
($88.9\%$~vs.\ $88.6\%$) and exceeds its FLUX object-removal UA
($97.3\%$~vs.\ $93.2\%$), without any model retraining. The same
recipe does not transfer to SD3.5; the diagnostic shows that object
identity is redundantly encoded across encoders and that effective
intervention requires hooking the post-MLP modulation signal,
consistent with TRACE's transcoder-coverage finding. We treat SD3.5
as a limitation and direction for future work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Text-to-image diffusion models have moved from U-Net backbones to
\emph{Diffusion Transformers}
(DiTs)~\cite{esser2024scaling,fluxdev2024}, which process text and
image tokens jointly across deep transformer stacks. As these models
scale, so do concerns about the concepts they reproduce: copyrighted
artistic styles, identifiable individuals, sensitive content. Concept
unlearning aims to suppress specific concepts in the output
distribution. Most existing work targets U-Net diffusion models with
fine-tuning or weight
edits~\cite{gandikota2023erasing,kumari2023ablating,gandikota2024unified};
the few methods that target DiTs either retrain layers
(TRACE~\cite{tracepaper}) or were developed for cross-attention
designs that no longer hold under joint
attention~\cite{khrulkov2024casteer}.

Inference-time activation steering~\cite{turner2024steering} offers an
attractive alternative: it is training-free, lightweight, and operates
on activations alone. CASteer~\cite{khrulkov2024casteer} establishes
the diverse-prompt-pair recipe for diffusion models, but evaluates
primarily on cross-attention-based U-Nets. Whether the same approach
transfers cleanly to DiTs that route text through multiple encoders
and a joint-attention stack is, to our knowledge, an open question.

\paragraph{This work.} We treat each DiT as a graph of
text-conditioning \emph{subspaces}---raw CLIP-pooled embeddings,
post-MLP modulation signals, projected joint sequences---and ask, per
concept and per architecture, where the concept lives and where it
must be intervened on. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item A per-subspace activation-steering framework for FLUX and SD3.5
that exposes one $\beta$ knob per text-conditioning entry point,
allowing architecture-aware interventions.
\item A \emph{subspace diagnostic}: before tuning betas, we zero each
entry point in turn on a fixed prompt to identify which subspaces
carry the target concept. The diagnostic is the data on which the
steering recipe is designed, not assumed.
\item Empirical evidence that FLUX and SD3.5 differ qualitatively in
where concepts are encoded: FLUX admits entry-point steering, while
SD3.5 redundantly encodes object identity across multiple encoders
and additionally requires intervention on the modulation MLP's
\emph{output}, aligning with TRACE's transcoder-coverage
finding~\cite{tracepaper}.
\item A per-concept hyperparameter search that addresses the variance
of single-global-beta selection on UnlearnCanvas, evaluated with
TRACE-aligned LLaVA classification.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:bg}

\paragraph{DiT architectures.}
Both FLUX and SD3.5 use \emph{Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformer}
(MMDiT) blocks where text and image tokens are concatenated and
processed by joint attention~\cite{esser2024scaling}. Text reaches the
backbone through two paths: (i)~a \emph{pooled} embedding that drives
adaptive layer-norm (AdaLN) modulation across all blocks, and (ii)~a
\emph{sequence} embedding that participates in joint attention with
image tokens. The two architectures differ in important details. FLUX
uses CLIP-L pooled (768d) and T5 sequence; the first 19 blocks are
``double-stream'' with separate text and image streams, followed by 38
``single-stream'' joint blocks. SD3.5 concatenates two CLIP encoders
(L and G) into a 2048d pooled vector, projects CLIP-L+G and T5
together into a 333-token sequence (77 CLIP + 256 T5), and stacks 24
joint blocks. SD3.5 is not distilled and runs with classifier-free
guidance scale typically $\geq 4$; FLUX-schnell is distilled and runs
at guidance scale~$0$. These differences will reappear when we ask
which subspaces support clean steering.

\paragraph{Activation steering.}
In LLMs, activation steering~\cite{turner2024steering,
panickssery2024steering} controls high-level behavior by adding a
linear direction to hidden states. The direction is typically
estimated as the mean difference of hidden states between contrastive
prompts. CASteer~\cite{khrulkov2024casteer} extends this to
cross-attention U-Nets: 50 ImageNet classes serve as base contexts,
each paired as ``\texttt{class}, concept'' versus ``\texttt{class}'',
and the averaged cross-attention difference is subtracted at every
block and timestep.

\paragraph{Concept unlearning in diffusion models.}
Most prior methods rely on weight updates: ESD~\cite{gandikota2023erasing}
fine-tunes with negative prompts; UCE~\cite{gandikota2024unified}
proposes closed-form weight edits; LOCOEDIT~\cite{tracepaper} edits
specific layers identified via gradient
attribution. TRACE~\cite{tracepaper} replaces target layers with
trained transcoders for surgical concept suppression and reports the
strongest UA on FLUX and SD3.5; it explicitly notes
(App.~D.1) that, in SD3.5 and FLUX, ``despite operating on a pooled
representation, [intervening at] the modulation MLP'' is necessary in
addition to the projection layer for effective suppression. The
UnlearnCanvas benchmark~\cite{zhang2024unlearncanvas} provides a
standard 10-style $\times$ 20-object evaluation grid with three
metrics: \emph{Unlearning Accuracy} (UA, fraction of target images not
classified as target), \emph{In-domain Retain Accuracy} (IRA, accuracy
on same-domain non-target concepts), and \emph{Cross-domain Retain
Accuracy} (CRA, accuracy on the other axis).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We treat concept unlearning in a DiT as the problem of identifying and
neutralizing each \emph{text-conditioning entry point} through which a
target concept reaches the joint-attention stack. An entry point is a
module that ingests text-encoder output and emits an activation read by
every downstream transformer block. We register hooks at these modules,
estimate a per-concept direction in their activation space using a
CASteer-style contrastive recipe, and subtract along that direction at
inference. No model weights are updated.

\subsection{Per-architecture entry points}
\label{sec:method_subspaces}

\paragraph{FLUX.} The two entry points are
\texttt{time\_text\_embed} (consuming the 768-d CLIP-L pooled
embedding and emitting the AdaLN modulation signal that conditions all
57 blocks) and \texttt{context\_embedder} (a $4096{\to}3072$ projection
of the T5 sequence into the joint-attention token space). We expose
two steering surfaces accordingly: a CLIP direction $\hat v_{\mathrm{CLIP}}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{768}$
applied as a pre-hook on the input of \texttt{time\_text\_embed}, and
a T5 direction operating on \texttt{context\_embedder}. For style we
use a single $4096$-d direction on the \emph{input} of \texttt{context\_embedder}
(raw T5 embeddings, mask-aware mean-pooled across tokens). For objects
we use a $3072$-d \emph{per-step} direction on the \emph{output} of
\texttt{context\_embedder}, learned at every denoising step. The
choice between input-side and output-side T5 hooks is empirical:
input-side gives a clean rank-one style direction once averaged across
contexts, while a per-step output-side intervention is required to
suppress object identity in the few denoising steps over which it is
committed (\S\ref{sec:exp_flux_obj}).

\paragraph{SD3.5.} SD3.5 concatenates two CLIP encoders into a $2048$-d
pooled vector and projects CLIP-L+G and T5 jointly into a $333$-token
sequence ($77$ CLIP $+\,256$ T5). We expose five subspaces:
\texttt{pooled\_clipL} (slice $[0{:}768]$), \texttt{pooled\_clipG}
(slice $[768{:}2048]$), \texttt{tte\_out} (the \emph{post}-SiLU output
of \texttt{time\_text\_embed} at dimension \texttt{caption\_proj\_dim}),
\texttt{ctx\_clip} (first $77$ tokens of \texttt{context\_embedder}
output), and \texttt{ctx\_t5} (tokens $77$--$333$). \texttt{tte\_out}
is included because TRACE explicitly reports that ``despite operating
on a pooled representation, [intervening at] the modulation MLP'' is
necessary in SD3.5 and FLUX in addition to the projection
layer~\cite{tracepaper}; our own zeroing diagnostic on SD3.5
(\S\ref{sec:exp_sd35}) corroborates this.

\subsection{Direction estimation}
\label{sec:method_directions}

For each target concept $c$ we generate $N{=}50$ contrastive prompt
pairs $\{(p^+_i, p^-_i)\}$ using $50$ ImageNet classes as base
contexts, following CASteer's diversification
recipe~\cite{khrulkov2024casteer}. For an object target,
$(p^+_i, p^-_i){=}(\text{``\texttt{class} with \texttt{concept}}'',\,
\text{``\texttt{class}}'')$; for a style target,
$(p^+_i, p^-_i){=}(\text{``\texttt{class},\,\texttt{concept} style}'',\,
\text{``\texttt{class}}'')$. Averaging across diverse base contexts
cancels prompt-specific layout/composition signals and isolates the
target-concept direction.

\paragraph{FLUX (style; \emph{pincer-v2}).} We bypass the diffusion
pipeline and read embeddings directly: for each pair we extract the
CLIP-L pooled embedding via the pipeline's \texttt{\_get\_clip\_prompt\_embeds}
and the T5 token sequence via \texttt{\_get\_t5\_prompt\_embeds}. The
T5 sequence is reduced to a single vector by mask-aware mean-pooling
(padding tokens excluded). Per-pair differences are accumulated, the
mean is taken, and the result is L2-normalized to produce
$\hat v_{\mathrm{CLIP}}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{768}$ and
$\hat v_{\mathrm{T5}}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{4096}$. The procedure yields
\emph{two} vectors per concept; no per-step factorization.

\paragraph{FLUX (object; \emph{pincer-perstep}).} CLIP is estimated as
above. For T5 we run the pipeline twice per pair (positive and
negative prompt at the same seed) with a forward hook on
\texttt{context\_embedder}'s output; at each denoising step we
mask-aware mean-pool the post-projection $3072$-d activation and
accumulate the per-step difference. After $N$ pairs the per-step
differences are averaged across pairs and L2-normalized, yielding one
direction per step. The total cache is one CLIP direction plus
$N_{\text{steps}}$ T5 directions. Per-step T5 is empirically required
because a time-averaged direction under-removes object identity in the
$4$-step schnell schedule.

\subsection{Inference-time injection}
\label{sec:method_inject}

For activation $h_S$ at subspace $S$ with direction $\hat v_S$ and
strength $\beta_S\!\geq\!0$, we apply the CASteer clip-negative rule:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:steer}
h'_S \;=\; h_S \,-\, \beta_S \cdot \mathrm{ReLU}\!\left(\langle h_S, \hat v_S\rangle\right) \cdot \hat v_S.
\end{equation}
The $\mathrm{ReLU}$ clamp ensures the intervention only fires when the
activation is positively aligned with the concept direction, avoiding
sign-flip amplification on unrelated prompts. For sequence-valued
subspaces (T5, ctx) the projection is computed per token; an optional
top-fraction gate restricts subtraction to the tokens with the highest
projections, providing token-level localization for object steering.
A step-range gate $[t_a, t_b)$ further restricts firing to a
contiguous window of denoising steps.

\paragraph{CFG-awareness.} Steering applies \emph{only} to the
conditional batch position. SD3.5 runs at guidance scale $4.0$;
modifying the unconditional reference frame would contaminate the CFG
extrapolation and partially undo the intervention, so we leave it
untouched. FLUX-schnell is distilled and runs at guidance scale $0$,
so the conditional position is the entire forward.

\paragraph{CLIP cap.} The CLIP entry point modulates every block via
AdaLN; large $\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}}$ destabilizes the whole image. We
expose a parameter $\kappa$ (\texttt{clip\_cap}) that caps the
effective scale at $\min(\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}}, \kappa)\cdot\mathrm{ReLU}(\cdot)$.
For style on FLUX we use $\kappa{=}1.0$ (typically with
$\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}}{=}0$, so the cap is inert); for object
unlearning on FLUX we set $\kappa{=}\infty$ (uncapped), which allows
$\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}}\!>\!1$ to push the projection through zero into
anti-concept space---necessary because object identity occupies a
multi-dimensional CLIP subspace, and a $1{\times}$-projection
subtraction merely slides the model to a neighbouring class.

\subsection{Subspace diagnostic}
\label{sec:method_diag}

Before tuning $\beta$, we run a fixed prompt $\langle\text{object},\text{style}\rangle$
once as baseline and once per subspace with that subspace's activation
zeroed on the conditional position. The resulting panel exposes which
subspaces carry which information; the steering recipe is read off
this panel rather than assumed. The diagnostic is the empirical anchor
for both FLUX (\S\ref{sec:exp_flux_diag}) and SD3.5 (\S\ref{sec:exp_sd35}).

\subsection{Per-concept hyperparameter selection}
\label{sec:method_hp}

Empirically, no global $\beta$ optimizes UA, IRA, and CRA jointly
across all $20$ objects and $10$ styles: at $\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}}{=}3,
\beta_{\mathrm{T5}}{=}5$ on FLUX, distinctive silhouettes
(Architectures, Bears) over-steer to UA${=}100\%$ but CRA${\sim}30\%$,
while broad scene concepts (Butterfly, Flame, Flowers) under-steer to
UA${<}5\%$. We therefore run a small per-concept grid search on a
$12$-image proxy set ($3$ proxy styles ${\times}\,2$ proxy objects
${\times}\,2$ seeds, with the target concept always on the relevant
axis). For each candidate $\beta$ we generate the proxy with steering
applied, classify with LLaVA, and score using a symmetric composite
\[
\mathrm{score}(\beta) \;=\; 0.5\cdot\mathrm{UA} \,+\, 0.25\cdot\mathrm{IRA} \,+\, 0.25\cdot\mathrm{CRA}.
\]
The weighting is the same for both targets: UA dominant because it is
the headline metric, IRA and CRA equal because UnlearnCanvas explicitly
shows that the two retention metrics dissociate, and a single
weighting keeps the methodology consistent across object and style.
The object grid sweeps
$\{(1{,}3),\,(3{,}5),\,(5{,}8),\,(8{,}12),\,(10{,}15)\}$ in
$(\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}},\beta_{\mathrm{T5}})$; the style grid sweeps
$\beta_{\mathrm{T5}}\!\in\!\{1,2,4,6\}$ at $\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}}{=}0$.
The selected per-concept $\beta$ is cached and reused for the full
benchmark.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:method_eval}

\paragraph{Benchmark.} We follow UnlearnCanvas~\cite{zhang2024unlearncanvas},
restricted to the $10$-style $\times\,20$-object grid used by
TRACE~\cite{tracepaper} for direct comparison. The prompt template is
``A \texttt{\{object\}} image in \texttt{\{style\}} style.''.

\paragraph{Classifier.} We use the LLaVA-1.6-Vicuna-7B classifier with
the numbered-list multiple-choice prompt format introduced by TRACE
(App.~E.4); the original UnlearnCanvas SD-1.5 classifier is unsuitable
because it generalizes below $6\%$ accuracy to FLUX and SD3.5 outputs.
LLaVA classifies each generated image into one of the $10$ styles and
into one of the $20$ objects.

\paragraph{Seeds and grid size.} We evaluate at the five TRACE
reproducibility seeds $\{188, 288, 588, 688, 888\}$. For each target
concept we generate $10\!\times\!20\!\times\!5{=}1000$ images.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
\emph{Unlearning Accuracy} (UA) is the fraction of \emph{target-bearing}
images whose target axis is not classified as the target.
\emph{In-domain Retain Accuracy} (IRA) is the LLaVA accuracy on the
target axis for non-target prompts (e.g., on style accuracy when the
target is Van Gogh, evaluated on images whose intended style is one of
the other nine). \emph{Cross-domain Retain Accuracy} (CRA) is the
accuracy on the orthogonal axis. We additionally report FID against a
shared $1000$-image unsteered baseline grid generated once and reused
across all concepts, and the CLIP-ViT-L/14 text-image score on the
generation prompt.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

We focus our experiments on FLUX.1-schnell, the $4$-step distilled
variant of FLUX.1 that runs at guidance scale~$0$. The full FLUX-dev
$28$-step model is gated and slower by ${\sim}7{\times}$; schnell is
license-permissive and matches the inference-time, training-free
spirit of activation steering. SD3.5-medium is run at guidance
scale~$4.0$ with $28$ denoising steps. All steering hooks operate
exclusively on the conditional batch position. Vectors for a concept
are learned in approximately one hundred forward passes
($N{=}50$ pairs $\times\,2$ prompts) and then cached.

\subsection{FLUX: subspace diagnostic}
\label{sec:exp_flux_diag}

On the prompt ``a Dog in Van Gogh style'', zeroing
\texttt{time\_text\_embed}'s input on the conditional batch
position (i.e., suppressing the CLIP-pooled signal) collapses the
generation to high-frequency noise; zeroing \texttt{context\_embedder}
preserves a recognizable dog and shifts the style. The reading is
unambiguous in FLUX: object identity is gated through CLIP-pooled
$\to$ AdaLN modulation, style through T5 $\to$ joint attention. The
finding is consistent with prior FLUX architecture
analyses~\cite{greenberg2025flux} and motivates our two-mode design:
CLIP-led for objects, T5-led for styles.

\subsection{FLUX: object unlearning}
\label{sec:exp_flux_obj}

Object steering uses \emph{pincer-perstep}: a CLIP-L pooled direction
($768$-d, single per concept) plus a per-step T5 direction at
\texttt{context\_embedder} output ($3072$-d, one per denoising step).
We start from the global default
$(\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}},\beta_{\mathrm{T5}}){=}(3,5)$ with
\texttt{clip\_cap}${=}\infty$ and step range
$[0, N_{\text{steps}})$, then run the per-concept grid search of
\S\ref{sec:method_hp}. Because schnell commits object identity within
$4$ steps, omitting either prong reproduces the failure mode
documented in our diagnostic notebooks: CLIP-only leaves a partial
silhouette (the model rolls to a similar class), while T5-only fails
to dent identity. The per-step T5 capture is the operative difference
from style mode and is necessary for objects with strong scene priors
(Flame, Flowers). Numerical results are reported in
\S\ref{sec:results_obj}.

\subsection{FLUX: style unlearning}
\label{sec:exp_flux_style}

Style steering uses \emph{pincer-v2}: a single $4096$-d T5 direction
on the input of \texttt{context\_embedder}, with
$\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}}{=}0$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{T5}}\!\in\![1,6]$
selected per concept. CLIP is intentionally disabled: in the FLUX
diagnostic, style does not flow through CLIP, and engaging the
$\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}}$ axis only depresses CRA without improving UA.
The T5 direction is time-averaged because, for style, contributions
from individual steps are correlated; a single rank-one direction
suffices once averaged across $50$ contexts.

\subsection{SD3.5: preliminary findings}
\label{sec:exp_sd35}

SD3.5 did not yield clean concept removal in our setup, and the
evaluation results below are reported only for FLUX. We summarize the
qualitative diagnostics to ground the limitations discussion.
On the same fixed prompt, no single zeroing destroyed the dog: zeroing
the full $2048$-d pooled vector left a recognizable (if abstracted)
dog, with $96\%$ of pixels changed; the CLIP-L slice alone changed
$36\%$ of pixels and the CLIP-G slice $77\%$, but in both cases the
animal identity persisted. Object identity in SD3.5 is therefore
\emph{redundantly} encoded across encoders rather than gated through a
single CLIP path. Style was localized: zeroing the CLIP region of
\texttt{context\_embedder} output destroyed Van Gogh on a held-out
prompt, while zeroing the T5 region left it intact. Joint
multi-subspace steering across the four input-side subspaces
(\texttt{pooled\_clipL}, \texttt{pooled\_clipG}, \texttt{ctx\_clip},
\texttt{ctx\_t5}) at low and moderate strengths failed to remove
objects; pushing strengths higher collapsed the image structure but
often left residual object features embedded in the new structure,
suggesting the joint-attention stack reconstructs concept content from
signal that survives entry-point subtraction. Following TRACE's
App.~D.1 finding~\cite{tracepaper}, we additionally hooked
\texttt{tte\_out} (the post-SiLU output of \texttt{time\_text\_embed},
i.e., the AdaLN modulation signal feeding every block); the hook is
implemented and the diagnostic confirms it acts on the source of
modulation, but achieving FLUX-comparable UnlearnCanvas numbers on
SD3.5 was not feasible within the project window. We treat SD3.5 as a
limitation (\S\ref{sec:discuss}) and an explicit target for future
work.

\subsection{Ablations on FLUX}
\label{sec:exp_ablations}

\paragraph{Number of contrastive pairs.} CASteer reports saturation
near $N{=}50$~\cite{khrulkov2024casteer}. We confirm that direction
quality is visibly noisier at $N{=}10$ on FLUX. We use $N{=}50$ for
all reported runs.

\paragraph{Composite objective.} Replacing the
$0.5{:}0.25{:}0.25$ composite with a UA-only objective during
per-concept search selects configurations that destroy retention
(CRA collapses on distinctive-silhouette objects); a UA${+}$IRA
objective under-selects on objects where CRA is the more pressing
failure mode. The symmetric composite produces selections closer to
the visual sweet spot in our review.

\paragraph{Step range and CLIP cap.} For style we fire across the full
$[0, N_{\text{steps}})$ window because style is painted throughout
denoising. For objects we observed during development that capping
$\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}}$ at $1$ silently throttles steering---subtracting
$1\!\times\!\hat v_{\mathrm{CLIP}}$ zeroes one axis of pooled CLIP, but
object identity occupies a multi-dimensional subspace, so the model
slides to a neighbouring class instead of failing. Removing the cap
($c{=}\infty$) and using $\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}}{=}3$ pushes the
projection through zero into anti-concept space and is what produces
clean object removal.

\paragraph{Boundary diagnostics.} For roughly $15\%$ of FLUX object
concepts (e.g., Flame, Butterfly, Flowers), the per-concept search
selects the boundary of the $5$-cell grid, indicating that the true
optimum lies outside the searched range. We log these as
``boundary-warning'' picks; extending the grid resolves them at the
cost of additional search compute.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We report the full UnlearnCanvas-style table for FLUX-schnell using
the per-concept $\beta$ selections from \S\ref{sec:method_hp}.
Following TRACE, all numbers are computed with the LLaVA-1.6-Vicuna-7B
classifier over $1000$ images per target concept, FID against a shared
$1000$-image unsteered baseline grid, and CLIP-ViT-L/14 score against
the generation prompt.

\subsection{Object unlearning on FLUX-schnell}
\label{sec:results_obj}

Across all $20$ object concepts the average UA is
$\mathbf{97.3\%}$ with average IRA${=}88.45\%$ and average
CRA${=}33.82\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:obj}). Fifteen of the twenty objects
reach UA${=}100\%$; the lowest UA values are on Human ($86\%$) and Sea
($86\%$), where the LLaVA classifier confounds with adjacent
categories. IRA stays high ($85$--$92\%$), confirming that
non-target objects in the same domain are preserved. The cross-domain
metric (CRA${=}33.82\%$) is lower than TRACE's reported FLUX object
CRA of $96.6\%$~\cite{tracepaper}; this is consistent with a steering
cost we explicitly accept: pushing the CLIP-pooled projection past
zero strongly suppresses object identity but partially scrambles the
AdaLN-modulated style signal that propagates through the same path.
Our object UA, by contrast, exceeds TRACE's reported FLUX object UA
($93.2\%$). Average FID against the unsteered baseline is $45.95$ and
average CLIP score is $0.260$; per-concept FID variation
($31.6$--$64.4$) tracks the prompt-level disruption introduced by
removing scene-anchored objects (Sea, Sandwiches: low FID; Frogs,
Waterfalls: high FID).

\subsection{Style unlearning on FLUX-schnell}
\label{sec:results_style}

Across the ten TRACE-aligned styles (Table~\ref{tab:style}), the
average UA is $\mathbf{88.9\%}$ with average IRA${=}35.72\%$ and
average CRA${=}86.6\%$. The headline UA is statistically on par with
TRACE's reported FLUX style UA of $88.6\%$~\cite{tracepaper}, achieved
without any model retraining or transcoder fitting. Average IRA
($35.72\%$) is at the structural ceiling reported by both TRACE
($36.1\%$) and the LLaVA-classifier-over-FLUX setup more broadly: when
one style is removed, the classifier's conditional accuracy on the
remaining nine drops because the LLaVA classifier's style
discrimination is itself imperfect, and the steered model produces
outputs that are stylistically more neutral. Average CRA ($86.6\%$)
indicates that object identity is largely preserved across style
unlearning; this is meaningfully lower than TRACE ($96.4\%$) but
substantially above the LOCOEDIT and UCE baselines reported in TRACE
Table~$1$ ($83.4\%$ and $76.6\%$, respectively).

Style results are not uniform. Cubism, Winter, Pop\_Art, and Ukiyoe
each reach UA${=}100\%$ on schnell; Impressionism is the weakest
(UA${=}66\%$), consistent with its visual closeness to a generic
realistic palette. Average FID is $53.81$ (range $29.7$--$75.2$) and
average CLIP score is $0.246$; the lower CLIP scores on
Cubism/Winter/Ukiyoe correlate with their high UA, reflecting the
content-level departure from the prompt's stylistic intent that is the
explicit goal of style unlearning.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discuss}

The FLUX results carry a mechanistic interpretation rather than a
purely engineering one. The diagnostic of \S\ref{sec:exp_flux_diag}
isolates two text-conditioning pathways with disjoint roles---CLIP-pooled
${\to}$ AdaLN modulation for object identity and T5 ${\to}$ joint
attention for style---and the two-mode design of \S\ref{sec:method}
follows directly from that observation. FLUX's combination of
distilled inference (no CFG amplification) and double-stream attention
allows the entry-point subtraction to propagate through the AdaLN
modulation pathway with minimal scrambling, which is what makes
training-free entry-point steering competitive with transcoder-based
weight editing on UA.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\emph{(i) SD3.5.} The same recipe did not yield clean concept removal
on SD3.5-medium. The diagnostic shows two reasons: object identity is
\emph{redundantly} encoded across CLIP-L, CLIP-G, and the T5 path, so
no single entry point dominates; and the joint-attention stack
reconstructs concept features from signal that survives input-side
subtraction. The \texttt{tte\_out} hook on the post-MLP modulation
signal---motivated by TRACE App.~D.1~\cite{tracepaper}---is
implemented and confirmed by the zeroing diagnostic to act on the
correct surface, but achieving FLUX-comparable UnlearnCanvas numbers
on SD3.5 was not feasible within the project window. We treat SD3.5
as the headline open problem and target it explicitly in future work.
\emph{(ii) FLUX-schnell.} We evaluate on FLUX.1-schnell ($4$ steps,
guidance scale~$0$) rather than FLUX.1-dev ($28$ steps, guidance
scale~$3.5$); schnell is license-permissive and faster, but the lower
step count and absence of CFG simplify entry-point steering and are a
confound when comparing absolute UA against TRACE's published FLUX
row. \emph{(iii) Cross-domain retain on objects.} Object steering in
our setup pushes the CLIP-pooled projection past zero, which produces
high UA but partially scrambles the AdaLN-modulated style signal that
shares the path; CRA on the orthogonal axis is correspondingly lower
than for transcoder-based methods. \emph{(iv) Rank-1 directions.} The
contrastive-pair direction is rank~$1$ by construction; distributed
concepts may benefit from rank-$k$ subspace removal as in
CASteer~\cite{khrulkov2024casteer}, which we did not evaluate.

\paragraph{Outlook.} The most direct extension is closing the SD3.5
gap. Two avenues look promising: tuning the \texttt{tte\_out} hook
into a competitive recipe (it is implemented but not tuned to a
benchmark sweep), and the DiT analog of CASteer's per-block
cross-attention hooks---steering the text-side $\mathrm{add\_k\_proj}$
and $\mathrm{add\_q\_proj}$ projections at every transformer block,
which addresses the redundancy our SD3.5 diagnostic exposes. A second
direction is implicit concept suppression: scene-grounded contrastive
pairs can target concepts that appear via statistical priors but are
not named in the prompt, suggesting an avenue for purging unwanted
biases from generative models without retraining.

\section{Implementation details}
\label{app:impl}

We use \texttt{FLUX.1-schnell} with 4 inference steps and
guidance scale~$0$, and \texttt{stable-diffusion-3.5-medium} with 28
inference steps and guidance scale~$4.0$ (lower than the default~$7$
to reduce CFG amplification of residual concept signal). All steering
hooks operate exclusively on the conditional batch position
under classifier-free guidance. LLaVA classifies into 10 styles and 20
objects with the TRACE-paper numbered-options prompt format. FID is
computed against a shared 1000-image unsteered baseline grid produced
once and reused across all concepts. The full reproducibility
configuration---including the per-concept $\beta$ JSON cache---is in
the code repository.
