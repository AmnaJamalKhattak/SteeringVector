\begin{abstract}
Activation steering controls model behavior by adding a learned
direction to hidden states; it is well-studied in LLMs and recently in
U-Net diffusion models, but has not been characterized for modern
\emph{Diffusion Transformers} (DiTs). We present a training-free
concept-unlearning framework for DiTs that intervenes at the
text-conditioning entry points: the CLIP-pooled embedding that drives
AdaLN modulation and the projected T5 sequence that enters joint
attention. A simple zeroing diagnostic separates the two roles in
FLUX---CLIP carries object identity, T5 carries style---and motivates
two protocols. \emph{Style-Vacuum} subtracts a single T5 direction;
\emph{Object-Vacuum} subtracts a per-step T5 direction together with
an uncapped CLIP direction. With per-concept $\beta$ tuning on
UnlearnCanvas and TRACE-aligned LLaVA classification on FLUX-schnell,
the method matches the published FLUX style-removal UA of TRACE
($88.9\%$ vs.\ $88.6\%$) and exceeds its FLUX object-removal UA
($97.3\%$ vs.\ $93.2\%$) without any retraining. The same recipe does
not transfer to SD3.5; the diagnostic shows object identity is
redundantly encoded across encoders, and effective intervention
appears to require hooking the post-MLP modulation signal, consistent
with TRACE's transcoder-coverage finding. SD3.5 is treated as a
limitation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Text-to-image diffusion models have moved from U-Net backbones to
\emph{Diffusion Transformers}
(DiTs)~\cite{esser2024scaling,fluxdev2024}, which process text and
image tokens jointly across deep transformer stacks. Concept
unlearning---suppressing specific concepts in the output
distribution---is a standard safety/IP requirement, but most existing
methods retrain
weights~\cite{gandikota2023erasing,kumari2023ablating,gandikota2024unified,tracepaper}
or were developed for cross-attention U-Nets that no longer hold under
joint attention~\cite{khrulkov2024casteer}. We ask whether
inference-time activation steering---which is training-free and
operates only on activations---can do the same job in a DiT.

Our answer for FLUX is yes. The key observation is mechanistic: FLUX
exposes two text-conditioning entry points, and each carries a
distinct concept type. A diagnostic that zeroes one entry point at a
time on a fixed prompt cleanly separates the roles: zeroing the
CLIP-pooled embedding produces noise, while zeroing the T5 path leaves
the object intact and shifts style. We design two protocols around
this finding---\emph{Style-Vacuum} and \emph{Object-Vacuum}---and
evaluate them on the UnlearnCanvas benchmark with the TRACE-paper
LLaVA classifier. The same recipe does not transfer to SD3.5; the
diagnostic shows why, and we report this as the main open problem.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i)~A two-protocol activation-steering framework for FLUX
(\textbf{Style-Vacuum}, \textbf{Object-Vacuum}) that intervenes only
at text-conditioning entry points and requires no model retraining.
(ii)~A subspace zeroing diagnostic that grounds the protocol design in
where the concept actually lives, applied to both FLUX and SD3.5.
(iii)~Per-concept $\beta$ selection via a 12-image proxy and a
symmetric UA/IRA/CRA composite, addressing the high concept-to-concept
variance of a single global $\beta$.
(iv)~Full UnlearnCanvas results on FLUX-schnell ($20$ objects,
$10$ styles), matching TRACE on style UA and exceeding it on object UA.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:bg}

\paragraph{DiT architectures.}
FLUX and SD3.5 use \emph{Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformer} blocks in
which text and image tokens are concatenated and processed by joint
attention~\cite{esser2024scaling}. Both models route text through two
parallel paths: a \emph{pooled} embedding that drives AdaLN modulation
across all blocks, and a \emph{sequence} embedding that participates
in joint attention with image tokens. FLUX uses a 768-d CLIP-L pooled
vector and a T5 sequence; the first 19 blocks are double-stream and
the next 38 are single-stream. SD3.5 concatenates two CLIP encoders
into a 2048-d pooled vector and projects CLIP-L+G and T5 into a
333-token sequence ($77$ CLIP $+\,256$ T5) processed by 24 joint
blocks. SD3.5 runs with classifier-free guidance (CFG) at scale
${\sim}4$; FLUX-schnell is distilled and runs at guidance scale~$0$.

\paragraph{Activation steering.}
In LLMs, activation steering adds a linear direction to hidden states
to control behavior~\cite{turner2024steering,panickssery2024steering}.
The direction is typically the mean difference of activations between
contrastive prompts. CASteer~\cite{khrulkov2024casteer} extends this
to cross-attention U-Nets using $50$ ImageNet base contexts to
diversify prompt pairs and a clip-negative ReLU rule for
clamping---both ingredients we adopt.

\paragraph{Concept unlearning.}
Most methods edit weights: ESD~\cite{gandikota2023erasing} fine-tunes
with negative prompts, UCE~\cite{gandikota2024unified} performs
closed-form weight edits, and TRACE~\cite{tracepaper} replaces the
projection layer and modulation MLP with trained transcoders, reporting
the strongest UA on FLUX and SD3.5. TRACE's App.~D.1 explicitly notes
that ``despite operating on a pooled representation,'' intervening at
the modulation MLP is necessary for effective suppression in SD3.5
and FLUX. UnlearnCanvas~\cite{zhang2024unlearncanvas} provides the
$10$-style $\times\,20$-object grid we evaluate on, with three
metrics: \emph{Unlearning Accuracy} (UA), \emph{In-domain Retain
Accuracy} (IRA), and \emph{Cross-domain Retain Accuracy} (CRA).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We treat unlearning as the problem of identifying and neutralizing the
text-conditioning \emph{entry points} through which a concept reaches
the joint-attention stack. We register hooks at these entry points,
estimate a per-concept direction in their activation space using
contrastive prompts, and subtract along that direction at inference.
No weights are updated.

\subsection{Subspace diagnostic}
\label{sec:method_diag}

Before designing any intervention, we run a zeroing panel: on a fixed
prompt $\langle\text{object},\text{style}\rangle$, we generate the
baseline once, then once per entry point with that entry point's
activation zeroed on the conditional batch position. The panel reveals
what each entry point carries.

For FLUX the reading is unambiguous. Zeroing
\texttt{time\_text\_embed}'s input (the CLIP-pooled embedding)
collapses the generation to high-frequency noise; zeroing
\texttt{context\_embedder} (the T5 projection) preserves a
recognizable object and merely shifts the style. Object identity is
gated through CLIP\,$\to$\,AdaLN modulation; style flows through
T5\,$\to$\,joint attention. The two protocols below follow directly.

\subsection{Direction estimation}
\label{sec:method_dirs}

For each concept $c$ we form $N{=}50$ contrastive prompt pairs using
$50$ ImageNet classes as base contexts, following
CASteer~\cite{khrulkov2024casteer}. For an object target,
$(p^+_i, p^-_i){=}(\text{``\texttt{class} with \texttt{concept}}'',\,
\text{``\texttt{class}}'')$; for a style target,
$(p^+_i, p^-_i){=}(\text{``\texttt{class},\,\texttt{concept} style}'',\,
\text{``\texttt{class}}'')$. Averaging across diverse contexts cancels
prompt-specific signal and isolates the concept direction. The
direction at entry point $S$ is the L2-normalized mean of
$h_S(p^+_i)\!-\!h_S(p^-_i)$ across pairs.

\subsection{Two protocols}
\label{sec:method_protocols}

\paragraph{Style-Vacuum.} A single 4096-d T5 direction is taken on the
\emph{input} of \texttt{context\_embedder} (raw T5 embeddings,
mask-aware mean-pooled across non-padding tokens), and a 768-d CLIP
direction on the input of \texttt{time\_text\_embed}. Two vectors per
concept; no per-step factorization. Defaults set
$\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}}{=}0$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{T5}}\!\in\![1,6]$
because the diagnostic places style in T5.

\paragraph{Object-Vacuum.} The CLIP direction is estimated as above.
For T5 we register a forward hook on the \emph{output} of
\texttt{context\_embedder} and accumulate per-step, mask-aware
mean-pooled differences over the denoising trajectory, yielding one
3072-d direction per step. Per-step is necessary because object
identity is committed within FLUX-schnell's $4$ steps, and a
time-averaged direction under-removes it. CLIP is intentionally
\emph{uncapped} ($\kappa{=}\infty$ below) so that
$\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}}\!>\!1$ pushes the projection past zero into
anti-concept space; capping at $1$ merely slides the model to a
neighbouring class.

\subsection{Inference-time injection}
\label{sec:method_inject}

For activation $h_S$, direction $\hat v_S$, and strength
$\beta_S\!\geq\!0$, we apply CASteer's clip-negative rule:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:steer}
h'_S \;=\; h_S \,-\, \min(\beta_S, \kappa)\cdot\mathrm{ReLU}\!\bigl(\langle h_S, \hat v_S\rangle\bigr) \cdot \hat v_S.
\end{equation}
The ReLU clamp ensures we only fire on positively aligned activations.
The cap $\kappa$ is per-protocol: $\kappa{=}1$ for Style-Vacuum (CLIP
is dormant anyway) and $\kappa{=}\infty$ for Object-Vacuum (uncapped).
For sequence-valued entry points (T5), the projection is per-token
and an optional top-fraction gate localizes subtraction to
concept-aligned tokens. Steering applies \emph{only} to the
conditional batch position; the unconditional CFG reference frame is
left untouched. FLUX-schnell runs at guidance scale $0$, so the
conditional position is the entire forward.

\subsection{Per-concept hyperparameter selection}
\label{sec:method_hp}

A single global $\beta$ is empirically suboptimal: at
$(\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}},\beta_{\mathrm{T5}}){=}(3,5)$ on FLUX,
distinctive silhouettes (Architectures, Bears) over-steer to
UA${=}100\%$ but CRA${\sim}30\%$, while broad concepts (Butterfly,
Flame) under-steer to UA${<}5\%$. We therefore run a per-concept grid
search on a $12$-image proxy ($3$ proxy styles
$\times\,2$ proxy objects $\times\,2$ seeds, with the target on the
relevant axis), classified with LLaVA, scored by
\[
\mathrm{score}(\beta) = 0.5\cdot\mathrm{UA} + 0.25\cdot\mathrm{IRA} + 0.25\cdot\mathrm{CRA}.
\]
The object grid sweeps $(\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}},\beta_{\mathrm{T5}})\!\in\!
\{(1{,}3),(3{,}5),(5{,}8),(8{,}12),(10{,}15)\}$; the style grid
sweeps $\beta_{\mathrm{T5}}\!\in\!\{1,2,4,6\}$ at
$\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}}{=}0$. Selected $\beta$'s are cached and reused
for the full benchmark.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:method_eval}

We use the UnlearnCanvas $10\!\times\!20$ grid restricted to the
TRACE-paper concept set, with the prompt template ``A
\texttt{\{object\}} image in \texttt{\{style\}} style.''. Generations
are classified by LLaVA-1.6-Vicuna-7B using the numbered-list format
introduced by TRACE (App.~E.4); the original UnlearnCanvas SD-1.5
classifier generalizes below $6\%$ to FLUX/SD3.5. We use the five
TRACE seeds $\{188, 288, 588, 688, 888\}$, generating $1000$ images
per target concept, and additionally report FID against a shared
$1000$-image unsteered baseline grid and the CLIP-ViT-L/14 score on
the generation prompt.

\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Experiments are on FLUX.1-schnell (4 steps, guidance~$0$).
Per-concept directions are learned in ${\sim}100$ forward passes
($N{=}50$ pairs $\times\,2$) and cached. Style-Vacuum is used for
style targets, Object-Vacuum for object targets, each with the
per-concept $\beta$ from \S\ref{sec:method_hp}. The headline numbers
below are computed on $1000$ images per concept. SD3.5-medium
(28 steps, guidance~$4.0$) is reported only as a diagnostic in
\S\ref{sec:exp_sd35}.

\subsection{Object unlearning on FLUX-schnell}
\label{sec:exp_obj}

$20$ object concepts, with average IRA${=}88.45\%$ (other-object
preservation) and average CRA${=}33.82\%$ (style preservation on
non-target images); see Table~\ref{tab:obj}. Fifteen of twenty
concepts reach UA${=}100\%$; the lowest UA values are on Human

categories. Average FID against the unsteered baseline is $45.95$ and
average CLIP score is $0.260$. Our object UA exceeds TRACE's
published FLUX object UA ($93.2\%$); CRA is lower
(\S\ref{sec:exp_compare}), which we discuss as an explicit trade-off.

\subsection{SD3.5: diagnostic, no clean removal}
\label{sec:exp_sd35}

The same recipe does not transfer. On the prompt ``a Dog in Van Gogh
style'', no single entry-point zeroing destroys the dog: zeroing the
full $2048$-d pooled vector changes $96\%$ of pixels but leaves a
recognizable animal; CLIP-L alone changes $36\%$, CLIP-G alone $77\%$,
and both leave object identity intact. Object identity is therefore
\emph{redundantly} encoded across the two CLIP encoders and the T5
path. Style is more localized: zeroing the CLIP region of
\texttt{context\_embedder} output destroys Van Gogh, while zeroing the
T5 region leaves it. Joint multi-subspace steering at moderate
strengths fails to remove objects; at high strengths the image
collapses but residual object features survive. Following TRACE
App.~D.1, we additionally hook \texttt{tte\_out}, the post-SiLU output
of \texttt{time\_text\_embed} (i.e., the AdaLN modulation signal); the
hook is implemented and the diagnostic confirms it acts on the right
surface, but achieving FLUX-comparable UnlearnCanvas numbers on SD3.5
was not feasible within the project window. We treat SD3.5 as a
limitation (\S\ref{sec:discuss}).

\subsection{Ablations on FLUX}
\label{sec:exp_abl}

\paragraph{Number of contrastive pairs.} Direction quality is visibly
noisier at $N{=}10$ and stabilizes by $N{=}50$, consistent with
CASteer's reported saturation~\cite{khrulkov2024casteer}.

\paragraph{CLIP cap.} Capping $\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}}$ at $1$ silently
throttles object steering: subtracting $1\!\times\!\hat v_{\mathrm{CLIP}}$
zeroes one axis of pooled CLIP, but object identity occupies a
multi-dimensional subspace, so the model rolls to a neighbouring
class. Removing the cap and using $\beta_{\mathrm{CLIP}}{=}3$ pushes
the projection through zero and is what produces clean object removal.

\paragraph{Composite objective.} Replacing the symmetric
$0.5{:}0.25{:}0.25$ composite with UA-only over-selects retention-killing
configurations; UA${+}$IRA under-selects on objects where CRA is the
more pressing failure mode. The symmetric composite is the operating
choice.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discuss}

The FLUX results carry a mechanistic reading: the diagnostic isolates
two text pathways with disjoint roles---CLIP\,$\to$\,AdaLN for object
identity, T5\,$\to$\,joint attention for style---and the two-protocol
design follows directly. FLUX's distilled inference (no CFG
amplification) and double-stream attention let entry-point
subtractions propagate through AdaLN with minimal scrambling, which is
why training-free steering is competitive with transcoder-based
weight editing on UA.

\paragraph{Limitations.} SD3.5 is the headline open problem: the
diagnostic shows redundancy of object identity across the two CLIP
encoders and the T5 path, and the joint-attention stack reconstructs
features from signal that survives input-side subtraction. The
\texttt{tte\_out} hook is implemented but a FLUX-level sweep on SD3.5
is left for future work. We evaluate on FLUX-schnell (4 steps,
guidance~$0$); the absence of CFG and lower step count simplify
entry-point steering and confound absolute comparisons to TRACE's
FLUX row, which was likely produced on the dev variant. Object CRA
is meaningfully lower than transcoder-based methods because pushing
the CLIP-pooled projection past zero scrambles the AdaLN-modulated
style signal that shares the path. The contrastive direction is
rank-$1$ by construction; rank-$k$ subspace
removal~\cite{khrulkov2024casteer} is an obvious extension.

\paragraph{Outlook.} The natural next step is closing the SD3.5 gap:
tuning the \texttt{tte\_out} recipe and adding the DiT analog of
CASteer's per-block hooks ($\mathrm{add\_k\_proj}$,
$\mathrm{add\_q\_proj}$) to address the diagnosed redundancy.

\section{Implementation details}
\label{app:impl}

We use \texttt{FLUX.1-schnell} (4 inference steps, guidance scale~$0$)
and \texttt{stable-diffusion-3.5-medium} (28 inference steps, guidance
scale~$4.0$). All steering hooks operate exclusively on the
conditional batch position. LLaVA classifies into the $10$ TRACE
styles and $20$ TRACE object classes with the numbered-list prompt
format. FID is computed against a shared $1000$-image unsteered
baseline grid produced once and reused across all concepts. The full
reproducibility configuration, including the per-concept $\beta$ JSON
cache, is in the code repository.
